# Red Lobster Crawls Out of Bankruptcy After Being Acquired by New Investors

Red Lobster has successfully emerged from Chapter 11 bankruptcy following court approval of its reorganization plan this week. The seafood restaurant chain has been acquired by RL Investor Holdings, a new entity backed by Fortress Investment Group along with co-investors TCW Private Credit and Blue Torch.

The transaction is expected to close by the end of September. Upon completion, Damola Adamolekun, the 35-year-old former CEO of P.F. Chang's, will assume leadership of Red Lobster. "This is a great day for Red Lobster," Adamolekun stated, noting the new investors have committed "more than $60 million in new funding" to strengthen the brand.

Jonathan Tibus, who guided the company through bankruptcy proceedings, will depart from his position. He expressed pride in Red Lobster's achievements during restructuring, stating the organization will emerge "stronger financially and operationally."

The chain will continue operating independently with approximately 30,000 employees and 544 U.S. and Canadian locations—a reduction from the 578 restaurants operating when the company filed for bankruptcy in May and the 650 it had in 2023.

Red Lobster's financial troubles stemmed largely from an unlimited shrimp promotion added to the permanent menu last summer, which generated over $11 million in operating losses during the third quarter of 2023. The company filed for bankruptcy citing estimated liabilities between $1 billion and $10 billion.
