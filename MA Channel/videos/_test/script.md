# Sample narration — the Disney–Pixar merger

## A partnership under strain

In January 2006, Disney announced it would acquire Pixar in an all-stock deal valued at about seven point four billion dollars. Pixar had made hits such as Toy Story and Finding Nemo. Disney had distributed those films, but the companies had disagreed over the terms of their relationship. [SOURCE: Disney acquisition announcement]

The purchase gave Disney Pixar's creative pipeline and brought its leaders into influential roles at Disney Animation. [VISUAL: two studios join on a timeline]

## What changed

Steve Jobs, Pixar's largest shareholder, became Disney's largest individual shareholder after the transaction. John Lasseter took a creative leadership role across the studios. Disney was buying a working animation culture as well as a library of characters.

That is the question every creative acquisition faces: can the buyer gain the talent without crushing the system that produced it? In this case, people mattered as much as the financial terms. Pixar needed room to keep making films in its distinct voice.
