# Deal Postmortem: script structure

Written by Opus from `dossier.md`. Target 3,000–4,000 words (20–27 min at 150 wpm).

## Format rules (the voice tool and storyboarder parse these)
- `## NN Section Name` starts each section; the section number drives VO file names.
- Narration is plain paragraphs, written for the ear.
- `[VISUAL: ...]` gives the storyboarder a hint; `[S#]` after a claim cites `sources/INDEX.md`. Both are stripped from the voiceover.
- `> note` lines are for the human reviewer only (e.g. `> OSCAR: add your take here`).

## Beat sheet
| # | Section | ~Words | Job |
|---|---|---|---|
| 00 | Cold open | 120–180 | The most striking fact/number + the question the video answers. No channel intro before the hook. |
| 01 | The golden age | 300–450 | Why people loved it and what it was worth. Makes the viewer care. |
| 02 | The pressure | 300–450 | Why a deal happened at all: activists, parent-company strategy, industry shift. |
| 03 | The deal | 500–700 | Mechanics in plain English: who paid what, how it was financed, what the fine print did. Signature doc-highlight moment. |
| 04 | The turn | 400–600 | The first cracks; cause and effect, with evidence. |
| 05 | The collapse | 400–600 | The ending, told concretely: closures, auctions, courtroom. |
| 06 | The verdict | 400–600 | Neutral scorecard: who made money, who lost, and which explanation the evidence supports. Address the popular myth. |
| 07 | The lesson | 150–250 | The deal principle the viewer can reuse, then a hand-off to the next video. |

> Insert one `> OSCAR:` prompt in 03 and in 06: that's where the practitioner's view goes (originality + credibility).

## Retention devices
- Open a loop in 00 and close it in 06.
- A mini-cliffhanger at the end of every section ("But the rent was only half the problem.").
- Every 60–90 s: a number, a document, or a reversal.
- Explain each piece of jargon once, in one sentence, the first time it appears.
